# Writing from a sealed research question

Before drafting, list each question, domain, population, comparison and limiting case supplied in the packet. For each, specify the outcome or statement you will investigate and the evidence or argument it would require. Include any data restrictions. Use those obligations to plan the paper.

When drafting, give every obligation a locator in the paper, a derivation or reproducible estimate when possible, and an explicit limitation when the inputs are insufficient. Test the scope of central statements at boundary cases. Check each substantive application as well as the general result. If the packet permits multiple defensible models, samples or interpretations, explain the choice and show how a plausible alternative changes what can be concluded. In the final pass, check the draft against your original list. Retain an open question wherever you cannot support a conclusion.
