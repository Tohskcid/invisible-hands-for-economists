# Blind independent-agent theory review — b02

**Recommendation: Reject at a top general-interest economics journal.** The paper gives a correct and clearly delimited fixed-point exercise under its imposed uniform-price institution, but it does not establish a distinct contribution or answer the decentralized simultaneous-contact question posed in the accompanying topic survey. This is an independent agent review, not a claim to be an external human referee. I read only the locked `main.tex`, `main.pdf`, `model.md`, `topic-survey-draft.md`, and `submission-lock.json`. I did not search for or inspect the putative published target, its abstract, any rubric, contamination log, or previous experiment.

## Six dimensions

| Dimension | Assessment |

| Novelty | **Insufficient for a top journal.** The manuscript itself disclaims novelty (`main.tex:26`), and its result reduces to a two-type uniform-price entry fixed point. The supplied survey reports a title-level direct match with an already published work (`topic-survey-draft.md:35-41`); this is a serious warning, **not** a verified theorem-level overlap finding. |

Thus the distinctive strategic effect of several simultaneous counterparties has been assumed away. |

| Logic | **Conditional claims are careful; economic extrapolation is limited.** The cross-contact welfare reversal compares different selected equilibria. The paper explicitly recognizes this; it supplies no selection mechanism predicting such a switch (`main.tex:93-97,174`). |
| Completeness | **Complete as a short benchmark, incomplete as a research article.** Seven-page PDF, derivations, boundary cases, example, and limitations are present. ## Findings, ranked

1. **Fatal for a top-journal submission: no established contribution or overlap boundary** (`main.tex:26,103`; `topic-survey-draft.md:35-41`). The manuscript presents itself as a historical reconstruction and says a source-level comparison is still needed. The survey records a direct title-level published match. I cannot infer the target paper's theorems from that warning, but a referee cannot credit originality or distinguish the present claims without a side-by-side comparison of primitives, equilibrium concept, propositions, and welfare results. The minimal remedy is to perform that comparison after unblinding and state a genuinely distinct theorem, if one exists.

2. **Major: the contact comparative static is entirely a match-probability comparative static** (`main.tex:37-45,71-74,101-105`). At any `n`, buyers offer the same exogenously imposed actuarially fair price; an entrant who has two available buyers receives exactly the same payoff as one with one available buyer. Consequently `n` enters the model only through `A_n`, and every equilibrium change follows from the entry-cost threshold `c_H+k_H/A_n`. This neither models nor tests competition among simultaneous counterparties, the mechanism that makes the survey's question difficult (`topic-survey-draft.md:3-5`). A top-journal version would need an endogenous offer or matching institution and show which current results survive it.

3. **Major: welfare claims have no predictive cross-equilibrium comparative static** (`main.tex:84-97,174`). The example switches from pooling at `n=1` to separation at `n=2` by fiat; the primitives contain no equilibrium selection rule that makes this transition occur. It establishes a logically valid nonordering of arbitrary selections, but cannot support a causal claim that more contacts lower welfare. The paper mostly states this limit accurately. A substantive welfare answer requires a justified selection or dynamic adjustment rule, or a clearly motivated correspondence-based criterion.

4. **Major qualification to “complete set”: exclusion of inactive-market outcomes rests on the equilibrium definition** (`main.tex:43-45,122-127`). The zero-profit price is defined as the mean value *among sellers who trade*, so it is undefined when nobody trades. The proof excludes a no-trade outcome on that basis and treats a deviating seller as facing a fair on-path price. Under a broader competitive or signaling equilibrium, off-path buyer beliefs and offers require specification; a no-trade configuration cannot be ruled out by this argument alone. This does not falsify Proposition 1 as a statement about the bespoke entry-price equilibrium, but the “complete” language should always retain that qualifier.

## Independent verification of the formal core

Set `A=A_n`, `z=c_H+k_H/A`, and `p(t)=(π_L v_L+π_H t v_H)/(π_L+π_H t)`. The maintained assumption implies every low type earns at least `A_1(v_L-c_L)-k_L>0` at any fair transaction price, so low types enter in every active-market equilibrium. With type-independent availability, transaction shares match entry shares. Differentiation gives `p'(t)=π_Lπ_H(v_H-v_L)/(π_L+π_Ht)^2>0`, with endpoints `v_L` and `v̄`. High-type entry has the sign of `p(t)-z` whenever trade is accepted. Hence separation iff `v_L≤z`, pooling iff `z≤v̄`, and the single distinct mixed root `t*=π_L(z-v_L)/[π_H(v_H-z)]` exists iff `v_L<z<v̄`. At either equality only the two pure outcomes remain as distinct equilibria. These checks agree with `main.tex:57-68,122-137`.

For `0<a<1`, `A_{n+1}-A_n=a(1-a)^n>0`, so `z` decreases; at `a=1` both are constant. Pooling feasibility is upward closed, separation feasibility downward closed, and the mixed threshold falls when present at both contact levels. These statements and the equality conventions in `main.tex:71-74,139-150` check out. The underlying presence of two pure equilibria follows from the increasing price in high-type participation; the interior root is an entry threshold, not an equilibrium selection prediction.

Writing `W(t)=A[π_L(v_L-c_L)+π_Ht(v_H-c_H)]-π_Lk_L-π_Htk_H`, pooling feasibility gives `A(v_H-c_H)-k_H>A(v̄-c_H)-k_H≥0`. Therefore `W(1)>W(t*)>W(0)` for a distinct interior root. At that root, substituting `k_H=A(z-c_H)` and `π_Ht*(v_H-z)=π_L(z-v_L)` yields `W(t*)=π_L[A(c_H-c_L)+k_H-k_L]`, strictly increasing with `A`. The pure-branch monotonicity also follows. These calculations confirm `main.tex:78-94,152-172`.

For the numerical example, exact arithmetic gives: `n=1`: `A=1/2`, `z=23/5`, `t*=13/17`, and `(W(0),W(t*),W(1))=(9/20,11/10,13/10)`; `n=2`: `A=3/4`, `z=61/15`, `t*=31/59`, and `(W(0),W(t*),W(1))=(7/10,59/40,87/40)`. Both `z` values lie strictly between `v_L=2` and `v̄=5`. Thus the reported `1.30>0.70` arbitrary-selection comparison is correct (`main.tex:97,174`), while every corresponding branch rises in this example.

**Disposition:** Reject as a top-journal research submission. The formal benchmark could be useful as a transparent teaching or replication note after its literature relationship is verified. This recommendation does not assert that the inaccessible target has any particular result.
