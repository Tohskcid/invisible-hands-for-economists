# Independent AI referee report on locked revision r02

**Submission:** *Allocating Heterogeneous Public Slots with Hidden Welfare Weights*  
**Target journal:** *Journal of Political Economy*  
**Recommendation:** Reject for this journal in its present form.  
**Review status:** Independent AI review, not external human peer review. I read only the public task, r02 lock and frozen manuscript, and my r01 report. The manuscript and PDF SHA-256 hashes match the scientific lock. I did not inspect the hidden target, rubric, comparison results, or unsealed files.

The two mathematical gaps identified in my r01 report are repaired. I find no new material error in the revised theorem or proof. My negative journal recommendation rests on the manuscript's unestablished novelty and limited economic scope, rather than a failure of the ironed-allocation result.

**Full-label conditional law: fixed.** Section 2 now assumes, for every observed vector of group labels, the exact conditional product law \(\bigotimes_i P_{g_i}\), with each factor depending only on the agent's own label. It also states that optimization is conditional on the observed vector. This excludes the label-dependence counterexample in my r01 review. The virtual-welfare proof and the Stieltjes ironing proof can now hold opponents' reports fixed while retaining the same \(f_g\) and \(\mu_g\). The bounded-subsidy argument uses the same condition correctly.

**Endpoint score and payment rule: fixed.** Sections 5–6 assign one-sided extended-real score limits at value endpoints, rank equal extended scores by fixed priority, and set endpoint charges through the envelope formula. Appendix A's ironing proof correctly treats endpoint report profiles as prior-null, while checking that the allocation remains monotone and its finite payment satisfies DSIC, IR, and no positive transfers on the *full* report domain. In particular, it avoids using an undefined product of an infinite score and zero quality as a welfare value. The construction covers the unbounded-score example raised in r01.

**Convex-minorant continuity: fixed.** Appendix A now proves endpoint continuity of the greatest convex minorant using its probability-measure representation, a modulus-of-continuity bound, and a mean constraint. Interior convexity plus endpoint continuity justifies the subsequent absolute-continuity and integration-by-parts steps. The contact-at-endpoints and affine-on-strict-ironing-interval properties remain valid. The sign of the Stieltjes inequality and its equality condition are correct: fixed tie priority makes an agent's quality constant wherever the minorant lies strictly below the raw primitive. Sorting positive ironed scores against descending slot qualities then attains the expected upper bound, including against randomized mechanisms (Proposition 4 and its Appendix A proof).

The weight-report wording remains appropriately limited to weakly supported truthful talk and almost-everywhere outcome equivalence (Section 3). The fully decreasing-score result and its direct covariance check remain sound (Proposition 5 and Appendix A). I therefore regard the allocation characterization as mathematically convincing under the now explicit assumptions.

For *JPE*, the contribution question remains open. The paper cites only Myerson (1981) and Saez and Stantcheva (2016), and expressly makes no priority or novelty claim (Introduction and Discussion). I cannot infer from this record that the theorem is already known, but the paper has not established what new mechanism-design insight its envelope, convex-minorant ironing, and quality-ranking combination adds to the literature. Its policy reach remains limited by a known group-specific prior and welfare criterion, independent one-dimensional private values, rival slots, and the absence of charge caps. The r02 repairs resolve the technical objections from my prior report; they do not, by themselves, make the submission ready for this journal.
