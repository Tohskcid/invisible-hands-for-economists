# Independent AI referee report

**Submission:** *Allocating Heterogeneous Public Slots with Hidden Welfare Weights*  
**Target journal:** *Journal of Political Economy*  
**Recommendation:** Reject in its current form for this journal.  
**Review status:** This is an independent AI review of the frozen, anonymized manuscript, not external human peer review. I used only the public task statement and the frozen manuscript. I did not inspect sealed evaluation material or search for the hidden target or additional literature.

The paper studies an assignable, rival-slot mechanism with private value and private welfare weight. Its strongest result is a conditional characterization: when each group's virtual score is nondecreasing, sorting agents by that score and using envelope payments maximizes the stated welfare objective among DSIC, ex post IR mechanisms without transfers to recipients (Section 5, Proposition 3). I find the core mathematics sound under the declared assumptions. The journal-level difficulty is contribution and scope, rather than an identified false theorem: the key identity is a direct envelope calculation, unequal qualities are handled by a rearrangement argument, and the paper deliberately leaves the nonregular problem open. The manuscript does not establish a distinct advance relative to the broader literature.

## Theoretical assessment

1. **Weight reports (Section 3, Proposition 1; Appendix A.1).** The two IC inequalities correctly make truthful utility equal across weight reports at a fixed value. Convexity then makes the expected quality and charge equal across such reports at almost every value. The atomless value law makes the exceptional set irrelevant to expected welfare. This is an outcome-equivalence result, however, not a literal impossibility of someone *saying* her weight: at a differentiability point, all weight reports are payoff-equivalent, so truthful cheap talk is one weakly dominant choice among others. The abstract, introduction, and conclusion should say that DSIC cannot *reliably elicit or use* the individual weight in outcomes, rather than suggesting that a report itself cannot contain the information.

2. **Virtual-welfare identity (Section 4, equations (3)–(4); Appendix A.2).** Substituting (p(v)=vQ(v)-\int_0^vQ(t)\,dt) into (\mu(v)(vQ-p)+\lambda p), then reversing the integral, gives exactly (S_g(v)=\lambda v-\lambda(1-F_g(v))/f_g(v)+\int_v^{\bar v_g}\mu_g(t)f_g(t)dt/f_g(v)). The finite value interval, bounded quality, and finite mean weight justify that interchange. Conditional independence across agents is doing real work here: it permits the same conditional weight and value distributions after fixing opponents' reports. The paper states this assumption and uses it correctly.

3. **Implementation and optimality (Section 5, Proposition 3; Appendix A.3).** Pairwise swaps establish the pointwise ranking for unequal qualities. A nondecreasing version of every score makes an agent's assigned quality weakly increase with her value, including at ties under fixed priority. The envelope payments then satisfy value IC, IR, and nonnegative charges. Since the manuscript's mechanism ignores weight reports, it also satisfies weight IC. The pointwise upper bound and its attainment prove optimality within the stated class. This proof does not cover arbitrary correlated types, charge caps, or nonregular scores, and the manuscript does not claim that it does.

4. **Checks on the extensions and examples (Section 5, bounded-subsidy subsection; Section 6; Appendices A.4–A.5).** A fixed subsidy changes the base utility (U(0)) by at most (B_i); its welfare coefficient is correctly (\bar\mu_g-\lambda). Thus the allocation stays the same when there is no shared budget. For the first example, (a=3,\lambda=1) yields (S(v)=2-v), so raising a report from (1/4) to (3/4) loses the slot against a (1/2) report and violates monotonicity. For the free-assignment example, (S(v)=1-v); its negative covariance with any nondecreasing own-value allocation gives the claimed (W\le1/2). These are valid counterexample and benchmark arguments.

## Contribution and exposition

The paper is unusually clear about its assumptions and claim limits. In particular, Section 2 distinguishes a ban on transfers *to* recipients from a cap on charges *from* them, and Section 7 acknowledges that the prior and the welfare criterion are normative inputs rather than identified data. Those distinctions make the result interpretable.

For this journal, the theoretical contribution needs more development. Section 4's score is an envelope transformation of the objective; Section 5's quality ordering is the standard exchange argument applied to that score. The private-weight observation is useful but largely follows from weights being absent from private utility. The two references in the manuscript, Myerson (1981) and Saez and Stantcheva (2016), motivate the ingredients but do not place this particular problem in the mechanism-design literature. The author explicitly disclaims a priority or novelty claim in Sections 1 and 7. I cannot infer from the supplied reference record that the result is already known, but the manuscript also gives a JPE reader no basis for judging what is new.

The incomplete nonregular case is consequential. Section 6 demonstrates that raw-score ranking can violate IC, yet offers no characterization, algorithm, or welfare comparison for that case. Even the constant-weight uniform example has falling scores whenever (a_g>2\lambda) (equation (6)), so this is not a remote pathology of the model. A general monotone-allocation solution, or a substantive new result about how hidden weights, groups, and unequal qualities interact outside regularity, would make the paper much stronger.

The model also has a narrow link to the motivating public-allocation problem. Group labels enter only through known conditional priors; the paper supplies no way to learn welfare weights or choose (\lambda). Transfers are unrestricted charges in the core model, and the slots are rival objects rather than a nonrival public good (Section 2). These are legitimate modeling choices, but they limit the force of any policy implication. The discussion properly recognizes most of them.

**Overall:** I would not recommend publication at *JPE* on this record. The conditional theorem and examples appear correct, and the manuscript would work as a concise technical note after tightening the weight-elicitation language. A journal-length contribution would need a sharper literature position and a substantial result beyond the regular score-ranking case.
