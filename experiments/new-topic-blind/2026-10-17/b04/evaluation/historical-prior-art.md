# b04 historical prior-art and time-isolation audit

**Result.** The frozen paper independently reconstructs a substantial part of the target's model, but its main virtual-welfare score and the economic meaning of hidden welfare weights were already explicit in the target's own 2020 working paper. That target version is **excluded** when judging the target's historical originality against other research. It is decisive, however, for the frozen paper's **October 2026 new-submission duplicate risk**, which is high. The frozen paper's finite-agent, dominant-strategy formulation and bounded individual subsidies are narrower variants whose independent novelty has not been established. This audit does not assign or change a content-reconstruction score.

## Dates and eligible corpora

The [publisher's History](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/728111) states “Published online: 10 May 2024”; volume 132(6) is dated 1 June 2024. Thus external work is eligible for the **historical novelty** comparison only if publicly available **strictly before 2024-05-10**. The [SSRN target record](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3609182) says “Posted: 18 Jun 2020” and “Last revised: 23 Jun 2023.” The SSRN page did not open directly in this audit (HTTP 403), but its indexed primary-record text reproduced those fields, and the sealed curator's [date evidence](unsealed/sealed/source-date-evidence.md) captured them. The [GRAPE-hosted 2020 paper](https://grape.org.pl/WP/40_Dworczak_website.pdf) has only a year on its cover; no earlier *precisely dated* public target version was established. Thus a strict author corpus needs public evidence **before 2020-06-18**. These are exclusive cutoffs; same-day items need an earlier verified timestamp, and uncertain dates overlapping a cutoff cannot be silently admitted.

The target's own SSRN/GRAPE draft, its [22 June 2023 author version](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.northwestern.edu/dist/1/2465/files/2023/06/Redistributive-Allocation-Mechanisms.pdf), and its 2024 journal text are versions of **one** research work. None is an external comparator for the target's historical originality. The 2023 PDF's cover date is not independent proof of the upload day; its classification as a target version makes that uncertainty immaterial. The 2020 target's first public date also matters for **priority**: an external paper published during 2020–24 cannot claim priority over results present in the 2020 target merely because it preceded the journal date.

These checked works show that the target did not originate incentive-compatible virtual values, the inability to elicit payoff-irrelevant traits, or the idea that rationing can serve equity. They do **not** by themselves reproduce its entire 2020 framework and heterogeneous-quality characterization. The limited record therefore supports a qualified assessment: the target combined and extended established methods in a distinctive one-sided setting, but this audit cannot certify a first-in-literature claim or a journal-level novelty threshold. Condorelli is the most serious external overlap among the checked full texts. The historical search was targeted, not exhaustive; other external work before 2024-05-10 could change that judgment.

## Direct comparison with the frozen manuscript

The frozen [manuscript](frozen-submission/paper/manuscript/main.tex) studies (u_i=v_iQ_i-p_i), an observed group, a latent welfare weight (w_i), heterogeneous qualities, nonnegative charges, and welfare (E\sum_i[w_i(v_iQ_i-p_i)+\lambda p_i]). The checked [2020 GRAPE target PDF](https://grape.org.pl/WP/40_Dworczak_website.pdf), pp. 7–12, already uses the same economic primitives and nonnegative transfers. Its p. 9 equation (2.6) sets (V_i(r)=\alpha J_i(r)+\Lambda_i(r)h_i(r)), with (J_i(r)=r-h_i(r)), (h_i(r)=(1-G_i(r))/g_i(r)), and (\Lambda_i(r)=E[\lambda_i(\tilde r)\mid\tilde r\ge r]). Under the substitutions (\alpha\mapsto\lambda), (r\mapsto v), and (\lambda_i(v)\mapsto\mu_g(v)), this is algebraically the frozen paper's score

\[
S_g(v)=\lambda v-\lambda\frac{1-F_g(v)}{f_g(v)}+\frac{\int_v^{\bar v_g}\mu_g(t)f_g(t)\,dt}{f_g(v)}.
\]

The target's 2020 model and §3 go further by solving general within-group nonmonotonicity with generalized ironing and allocating quality across groups. The [published text](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/728111), §§II.C–IV, makes the same points especially explicit: Claim 2 says reporting the payoff-irrelevant welfare weight cannot improve the objective; Claim 3 gives the weighted virtual value; Theorems 1–2 handle ironing and across-group quality assignment. The frozen paper's weight-report proposition, virtual-welfare identity, regular score ranking, and free/rationing examples therefore have strong *substantive* overlap, even though its proofs and finite-agent dominant-strategy implementation are phrased differently.

There are real formal differences. The target uses a unit mass of agents and interim incentive constraints; the frozen paper uses finitely many independent agents, ex post feasibility, and dominant-strategy incentives. Under monotone scores, its pointwise ranking and envelope payment give a valid finite-agent route to a restricted optimum. Its (p_i\ge-B_i) extension permits a bounded fixed subsidy, whereas the target's nonnegative-transfer baseline and lower-type utility choice are different. Those distinctions prevent a claim of literal theorem identity. Yet the new paper's most prominent formula and mechanism insight are already in the target, and its regular case is materially less general than the target's ironing solution. No checked source establishes the finite-agent or bounded-subsidy variant as a new research contribution. Today's independent-submission **duplicate risk is high**; this is a judgment about the frozen 2026 manuscript, not a claim that the 2024 target lacked historical originality.

## Author-corpus and contamination findings

The frozen [source manifest](frozen-submission/literature/sources.json) and [handoff](frozen-submission/literature/safe-handoff.md) contain only Myerson (1981) and Saez–Stantcheva (January 2016). Both are safely before 2020-06-18 on verified publisher evidence. The [source preflight](frozen-submission/source-preflight.json) records `valid: true`, `author_corpus_safe: true`, and manifest SHA-256 `49645708bbb3dec68982eb4ed9026783b9db56738fd7da90ca9054b08827e499`. The final timeline's `author_material` flags agree. Condorelli (2013) and the 2018 BFI paper would also have been date-eligible, but are absent from the actual handoff; this is a **literature-coverage deficit**, not a time-cutoff breach.

The [contamination log](contamination-log.md) records target publisher/2023-version URLs appearing in a scout's search results at 2026-10-17 13:51:53 UTC, before the [submission lock](../submission-lock.json) at 13:57:55 UTC. The scout did not open those results or send their content to author agents; the lock records the title exposure. The [public hashes](../public-hashes.json) say the target archive was sealed before authoring. These records support procedural separation, but there was no technical filesystem fence or model-memory/web isolation. They cannot prove the author was unaware of the target. The unprovided but safe Condorelli/2018 work further limits what this run says about an author reasoning from the full eligible literature.

The [timeline](historical-timeline.json) was classified with `python3 scripts/check_historical_prior_art.py ... --require-author-safe`; the [check output](historical-timeline-check.json) reports a valid record, both author items safe, all target versions excluded, and each external item before the journal cutoff. That script checks **date eligibility only**, not conceptual priority or novelty. The frozen manuscript, PDF, and source manifest SHA-256 values still match the submission lock; this audit changed only evaluation files.
