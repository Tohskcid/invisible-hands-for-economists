The paper does not identify a dose-response curve for arbitrary minimum-wage changes. 6 Sensitivity and Adjustment Margins Sensitivity checks address specific measurement and composition threats. Alternative part-time weights change the mapping from headcounts to labor input. Excluding managers changes both the outcome and the complete-component sample. Omitting permanent closures conditions on survival and therefore addresses a different population; it is not preferred because it can remove part of a policy response. Interview-mode and shore restrictions examine reporting and seasonal-composition differences. Leave-one-chain-out estimates test dependence on a chain’s weight, and winsorization reduces the influence of extreme changes. Every variant is displayed without selecting on its standard error. Their magnitudes are not identical: omitting Burger King yields 1.55, while omitting KFC yields 3.56. Consequently, a single common effect across chains is not established. The closure-excluding contrast is 2.72. Stability of these signs cannot refute a shared differential state shock or measurement error common to multiple variants. A diagnostic compares Pennsylvania restaurants with the 25 New Jersey complete-pair restaurants whose initial wage is at least the new minimum. The contrast is -2.57 with HC1 standard error 2.12. This differs in direction from the full sample and is imprecise. High-wage establishments are a selected group, not an untreated experiment: wage spillovers and differing trends remain possible. The diagnostic therefore cautions against interpreting one uniform state effect and does not validate or reject parallel trends by itself.
