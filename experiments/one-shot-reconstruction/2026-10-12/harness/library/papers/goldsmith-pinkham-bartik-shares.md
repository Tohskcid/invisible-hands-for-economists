# Share-exogeneity interpretation of Bartik instruments

## Problem

- interpret what variation identifies a Bartik IV coefficient
- diagnose influential exposure shares and their exogeneity

## Assumptions

- the exposure-share identification framework developed in the paper
- valid share-level exclusion restrictions for the relevant shocks and controls
- first-stage relevance in the weighted components

## Conclusion

Under the paper's framework, the Bartik estimator can be represented through exposure-share instruments, making identifying variation and influential shares explicit.

## Method

Rotemberg-weight decomposition and share-level IV interpretation

## Failure conditions

- correlated or endogenous shares invalidate the share-exogeneity design
- negative or concentrated weights make aggregate diagnostics misleading
- shock exogeneity is a distinct alternative design and must not be silently substituted

## Scope note

State whether identification is asserted through shares or shocks; the assumptions are not interchangeable.
