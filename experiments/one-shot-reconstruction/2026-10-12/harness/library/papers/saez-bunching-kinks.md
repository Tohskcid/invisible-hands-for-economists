# Bunching identification at budget-set kinks

## Problem

- infer behavioral elasticities from excess mass near a tax kink
- construct a counterfactual income density absent the kink

## Assumptions

- a defensible smooth counterfactual density around the kink
- the behavioral model linking excess mass to the targeted elasticity
- stable reporting, optimization, and enforcement conditions relevant to the application

## Conclusion

Under the paper's behavioral and counterfactual-density assumptions, excess bunching near tax kinks can identify an elasticity of reported income.

## Method

counterfactual density estimation and excess-mass mapping

## Failure conditions

- round-number reporting, optimization frictions, or evasion can mimic or alter bunching
- counterfactual polynomial and excluded-region choices can drive excess mass
- heterogeneous responses may not map to one structural elasticity

## Scope note

Treat the density and behavioral mapping as identifying assumptions, not graphical facts.
