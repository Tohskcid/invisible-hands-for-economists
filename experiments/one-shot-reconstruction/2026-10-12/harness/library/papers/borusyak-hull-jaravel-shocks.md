# Shock-exogeneity framework for shift-share IV

## Problem

- identify shift-share IV effects using quasi-random shocks
- permit exposure shares to be endogenous under a shock-level design

## Assumptions

- quasi-random assignment of many shocks relative to shock-level unobservables
- shock-level relevance and regularity conditions from the paper
- controls capture relevant shock-level confounding in the required shift-share form

## Conclusion

SSIV orthogonality and coefficients can be represented by equivalent shock-level moments and regressions, supporting identification from quasi-random shocks under the stated conditions.

## Method

exposure-weighted aggregation and equivalent shock-level IV regression

## Failure conditions

- systematic shock confounding violates the design
- too few or highly concentrated shocks weaken identification and inference
- conventional unit-level standard errors need not reflect shock-level assignment

## Scope note

Map the sampling and assignment unit to shocks before choosing controls or inference.
